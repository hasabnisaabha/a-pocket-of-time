Remembering I have a bad sense of humor

*Channels my 14 year old self with her delusions of grandeur and main character syndrome*

So hey guys, as you might know, I had a blog. It had all of ... 42? Subscribers. One of which was probably my dad.

I had the pleasure of revisiting my blog and reading some of the... *shudders* posts.

I cringed the way you do at your unfinished self. But then I looked past the words I'd written. I'd forgotten how... earnest I was?

I was so direct. I had the audacity to think, I have thoughts. The internet exists. These two facts clearly belong together.

The thoughts themselves? I had no business nor credentials holding such dramatic opinions so confidently.

“Life after death”
“Temple run is life”
...

Bruh? I am thirteen and this is deep, much?

Then... randomly? What happens if you lick your dog?? ... are you okay, child?

I'd written a letter to Malala after reading her book “I am Malala”. I'd dreamt I was chatting with her over mutton biryani. And I had decided to post the letter over on my blog in case she stumbled upon my blog while browsing “the internet”.

As if random Nobel laureates go around like—

Malala
↓
randomly browsing WordPress one Tuesday

“Hmm... let's search: ‘Letter to Malala by Indian teenager 2016.’”

“Oh! There it is!”

A post titled “why I am an agnostic”. I'd written “I know I'll get hate comments, and I'm prepared for it” and I meant that with the utmost sincerity.

Like. Darling. Your readers consist of your proud father and other confused relatives who'd comment sleepily “nice beta” at most.

The internet, for me, or at least WordPress felt like a friendly neighborhood I guess? Where I'd browse random blogs, and I guess others would too?

One of those strangers I remember especially was ... ihaveabadsenseofhumor. I don't know who they were, but apparently they stumbled upon my blog, read one post, and left a comment... and decided to stay.

It felt like someone was binge reading my content. I'd briefly fantasised it was Malala, yes, sorry, I'll see myself out now.

I hope they know they accidentally became part of the reason this website exists.

WordPress felt like a small corner of the old internet where curious teenagers wandered into each other's blogs, left kind comments, talked about puppies, philosophy, poems, and biology, then wandered off again.

I suppose this website exists because of people like ihaveabadsenseofhumor. I don't know what they looked like. I don't know where they lived. I don't even know if they'd remember my blog if I somehow found them today.

But I remember that they read it. And for fourteen-year-old me, that mattered.

Maybe that's what I'm trying to rebuild.

So, here I am. A whole ass adult. Oddly emotional about the digital sticky notes a kind stranger left. Trying to replicate the kind of internet I remember.

If that's you...
