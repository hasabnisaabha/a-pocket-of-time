My first pocket of time

So...

My first pocket of time exists at my grandmother's house. (The old one, not the modern one she's moved to. This one doesn't seem to have the yellowed tinge of life to it at its edges.)

I used to be brave. I was brave. Every Saturday I'd promise. This time, this time I'll stay the whole night without crying. I'll not call my parents to come get me.

... I can't pinpoint the first weekend I succeeded. But I was brave. I tried again week after week.

The house had a small terrace- attached to a passage connecting it to kitchen. In mornings, I would be tasked with bringing the sun-shown utensils back to the kitchen.

I'd sit there, in the sun for hours. Observing my upside down reflection in the convexity of a spoon, and rainbow collecting in the steel pot, ready to spill as you poured it in the sun at just the right angle.

Afternoon came with clothes stand. Once all the utensils were in, clothes had to be shown sun. The stand was a hollow pyramid, with ladderlike rods on it. You'd open it, and hang clothes on the rods. I found the stand fascinating, especially when we'd dry bedsheets.

I'd have my aunt spread one bedsheet to cover the whole stand, and leave one side open, like a door. And I'd take my clothes and utensils and move in my new home.

Some days my aunt would bring her laptop in my home, and put on “my neighbour totoro” as we munched on some cut up fruit, me inside my home, her right outside it (she wouldn't fit, as even I had to crouch).

Then I had to take a mandatory nap, cause big girls HAVE to sleep in the afternoon, too.

Post which, I had some envelopes ready to put cards in. These were some important documents of my grandfather only I was entrusted with. I could do it for hours.

As the day would start to end, and it started getting dark, my grandparents would look at each other, and start to open the folding bed. When expanded, it could fit 2 adults and a coiled child between the 2 perfectly.

This time I'll sleep the whole night. I'd chatter as I would go to sleep.

And I'd sleep. Usually there'd be a tiger in the house. Or I'd be running from something. Or I'd be falling and falling and falling — and I'd wake up. Crying. I want to go home. Call my parents. I want my mom.

And I'd be home within 20 minutes, sleeping peacefully between my parents.

And next week, it'd be the same, like clockwork.

I was brave. I tried again week after week.
